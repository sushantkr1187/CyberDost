🔐 The eMudhra TLS Certificate Incident & CA Trust Infrastructure Audit

In 2024–2025, security research and CA/Browser Forum audits uncovered critical vulnerabilities in Certificate Authority (CA) issuance and cryptographic key generation processes at eMudhra. The incident highlighted that having an active HTTPS padlock is no guarantee of security. Flawed pseudo-random number generation (PRNG), predictable seeds, sub-CA control failures, and delayed Certificate Transparency (CT) logging can allow unauthorized certificates to be created for website impersonation.

1. Flawed CA Keygen & Weak Seeds

Subscriber keys or sub-CA roots generated using deficient randomness algorithms produce RSA primes vulnerable to GCD batch factorization or Coppersmith's attack (ROCA pattern).

2. Certificate Transparency Evasion

Failure to promptly append Signed Certificate Timestamps (SCTs) to public CT logs prevents domain owners from monitoring unauthorized certificate issuance in real-time.

3. CAA Record Absence

Without strict DNS Certification Authority Authorization (CAA) records, any compromised or flawed CA worldwide can issue certificates for high-value banking and government domains.
